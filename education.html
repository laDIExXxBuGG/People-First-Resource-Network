Considering homeschooling in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania has specific requirements for home education programs. Before withdrawing a child from school or beginning a program, read the current state guidance and confirm which procedures apply to your circumstances.

  • ☐ Read the current Pennsylvania Home Education and Private Tutoring guidance.
  • ☐ Confirm which school district handles the required documentation.
  • ☐ Review the applicable affidavit or unsworn declaration and educational objectives requirements.
  • ☐ Check the current rules for health documentation, educational records, evaluations, and deadlines.
  • ☐ Ask the district how to submit documents and keep copies of everything sent.
  • ☐ If the child has a disability or needs accommodations, ask the relevant education authority or a qualified advocate about applicable options.

Read the official Pennsylvania home education guidance

This checklist is a starting point, not a complete statement of Pennsylvania law. Requirements can vary with the circumstances and may change.

When a learner needs extra support

School is not working

Write down the learning barriers and examples of what is happening. Ask the school or relevant education authority about available support, evaluation, and problem-solving procedures.

A disability may affect learning

Keep notes about the learner's needs and ask which school team, agency, or qualified professional can explain evaluation and accommodation options.

Traditional education is not a fit

Compare options such as homeschooling, online learning, adult education, vocational programs, or alternative schedules. Check legal requirements and eligibility before making a change.

Education costs too much

Start with public libraries, free online lessons, publicly funded programs, and financial aid information before paying for courses or materials.

Build a learning support plan

Use these prompts in your own notes. You do not need to solve everything at once.

  1. What does the learner want or need to learn?
  2. What is making learning difficult right now?
  3. Which format works best: in person, online, audio, visual, hands-on, or a combination?
  4. Which free resources could help?
  5. Is an evaluation, accommodation, or specialist guidance needed?
  6. What is the next manageable step, and who can answer questions?

Helpful tip: Keep a simple record of questions, applications, important dates, contacts, and responses. It can make follow-up easier.

If the first option does not work

A closed door does not mean that learning has to stop.

  • Ask a public library about free materials and learning access.
  • Ask the school district or state education agency about official options and procedures.
  • Try free online learning before purchasing a course.
  • Ask providers about financial aid, fee waivers, or flexible schedules.
  • For disability-related concerns, consult the appropriate education authority or a qualified advocate.
