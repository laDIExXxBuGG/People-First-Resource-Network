Considering homeschooling in Pennsylvania?

Homeschooling rules vary by jurisdiction and may include formal notice, documentation, evaluation, or recordkeeping requirements. Do not rely on an old checklist or another family's experience as proof of current requirements.

  • ☐ Check current Pennsylvania homeschool requirements.
  • ☐ Confirm which school district or authority handles the required paperwork.
  • ☐ Check deadlines and the current version of any required forms.
  • ☐ Ask how educational records, evaluations, and attendance must be documented.
  • ☐ Keep copies of submitted forms and written responses.
  • ☐ If the learner has a disability or needs accommodations, ask the relevant agencies or qualified advocates about applicable options and limitations.

This is a preparation checklist, not a complete statement of Pennsylvania law. Verify each requirement with official sources.

When a learner needs extra support

School is not working

Write down the learning barriers, contact the school or relevant education authority, and ask what support or evaluation processes may be available.

Learning needs are not understood

Keep examples of the difficulty, ask which professional or school team can assess it, and request information about accessible learning approaches.

Traditional education is not a fit

Compare lawful options such as homeschooling, adult education, online learning, vocational programs, or alternative schedules. Requirements and eligibility differ.

Education costs too much

Check public libraries, free course materials, community programs, grants, and financial aid before paying for a course or service.

Your learning support plan

Use these prompts in your own notes to identify practical next steps.

  1. What does the learner want or need to learn?
  2. What is making learning difficult right now?
  3. What format works best: in person, online, audio, visual, or hands-on?
  4. What free resources are available?
  5. Does the learner need accommodations, evaluation, or specialist guidance?
  6. What is the next small step, and who can answer questions?

Find another way

If one program is unavailable, too expensive, or not a good fit, there may be another route.

  • Ask a public library about free materials and learning access.
  • Ask the school district or state education agency about official options.
  • Explore free online learning before purchasing a course.
  • Ask education providers about fee waivers, financial aid, or alternative schedules.
  • For disability-related questions, consult the appropriate education authority or a qualified advocate.
