Preparing to ask for disability support

  • ☐ Identify the specific barrier or problem you want help addressing.
  • ☐ Write down how it affects work, school, healthcare, transportation, housing, or daily activities.
  • ☐ Check the program's official eligibility and documentation requirements.
  • ☐ Keep copies of applications, letters, decisions, and important deadlines.
  • ☐ Ask which accessible communication methods are available, including online or written contact when offered.
  • ☐ Ask whether free assistance, an advocate, or an accommodation is available.
  • ☐ If denied, read the written explanation and check any appeal deadline promptly.

When access barriers get in the way

I cannot manage the process alone

Ask whether free application help, an advocate, a representative, or an accessible contact method is available.

I cannot travel easily

Ask the organization whether online, written, mail, telephone, or other accessible options exist. Availability varies by service.

I need an accommodation

Explain the barrier and what change could help you access the service. Ask about the accommodation process and any documentation requirements.

I do not qualify for one program

Ask about other programs or eligibility categories. If you believe a decision was incorrect, review the written notice and appeal instructions.

Your access and support worksheet

Use these prompts in your private notes to plan your next step.

  1. What service, task, or opportunity am I trying to access?
  2. What barrier is preventing access?
  3. What change or support could help?
  4. Which agency or organization is responsible?
  5. What documents or application are required?
  6. What accessible contact method can I use?
  7. What can I do if the first request is denied or unavailable?

Keep personal medical and benefit information private. Share sensitive documents only through appropriate, trusted channels.

Find another way forward

A barrier does not mean you should have to give up on getting support.

  • Contact Disability Rights Pennsylvania for information about disability-related rights and advocacy services.
  • Ask an independent living center about advocacy and community resources.
  • Check whether free benefits counseling or application assistance is available.
  • Request accessible communication or a reasonable accommodation when appropriate.
  • Keep written records of requests, responses, decisions, and deadlines.
