Preparing to ask for disability support

  • ☐ Identify the specific barrier you are trying to address.
  • ☐ Write down how it affects access to work, school, healthcare, transportation, housing, or daily activities.
  • ☐ Check the program's official eligibility and documentation requirements.
  • ☐ Keep copies of applications, letters, decisions, and deadlines.
  • ☐ Ask what accessible communication methods are available.
  • ☐ Ask whether free assistance, an advocate, or an accommodation is available.
  • ☐ If you receive a denial, review the written explanation and any appeal deadline promptly.

When access barriers get in the way

I cannot manage the process alone

Ask the agency whether free application help, a representative, an advocate, or an accessible way to communicate is available.

I cannot travel easily

Ask whether telephone, online, mail, home-based, or other accessible options exist. Availability varies by service.

I need an accommodation

Describe the barrier and the change that would help you access the service. Ask about the organization's accommodation process and documentation requirements.

I do not qualify for one program

Ask about other programs, different eligibility categories, local independent living centers, or an appeal if you believe the decision was incorrect.

Your access and support worksheet

Use these prompts in your private notes to plan your next step.

  1. What service, task, or opportunity am I trying to access?
  2. What barrier is preventing access?
  3. What change or support could help?
  4. Which agency or organization is responsible?
  5. What documentation or application is required?
  6. What accessible contact method can I use?
  7. What is the next step if the first request is denied or unavailable?

Keep personal medical and benefit information private. Share sensitive documents only through appropriate, trusted channels.

Find another way

A barrier does not mean you should have to give up on getting support.

  • Contact your state's protection and advocacy organization for disability-rights guidance.
  • Ask a local independent living center about advocacy and community resources.
  • Check whether free benefits counseling or application assistance is available.
  • Request accessible communication or reasonable accommodation when appropriate.
  • Keep written records of requests, responses, decisions, and deadlines.
