Start where you are

Whether you are changing careers, returning to learning, exploring work for the first time, or trying to find a path that fits your circumstances, you deserve clear information and options you can realistically pursue.

Make a plan that fits your circumstances

You do not need to have everything figured out before taking a first step. Use this checklist to prepare for a conversation with a training provider, workforce agency, school, or career counselor.

  • Identify one or two interests, skills, or types of work you want to explore.
  • Write down what you need to learn, improve, or have recognized.
  • Ask whether the program is free and whether there are fees for materials, exams, equipment, or transportation.
  • Ask about eligibility, required documents, application deadlines, and waitlists.
  • Check whether classes are online, in person, self-paced, or available at flexible times.
  • Ask about accommodations, accessible formats, remote participation, and disability-related support if needed.
  • Confirm whether completing the program leads to a recognized credential, license, qualification, or employer pathway.
  • Save the official link, contact details, requirements, and date you checked the information.

When the usual path does not work

If a program costs too much

Ask about fee waivers, scholarships, publicly funded training, workforce funding, library-based learning, and free introductory courses. Confirm the total cost before committing.

If you cannot attend in person

Ask about remote enrollment, online classes, phone or video appointments, mailed materials, flexible schedules, and available transportation assistance. Do not assume every provider offers these options.

If disability creates barriers

Explore vocational rehabilitation, workplace accommodations, accessible training, and disability-specific employment support. Ask about the application process and eligibility.

If you are not eligible or the program is full

Ask for the specific reason, the next enrollment date, an appeal or review process if available, and referrals to other programs with different eligibility requirements.

Your personal planning worksheet

You can copy these prompts into your own notes. You do not need to enter private information on this website.

  1. My goal: What would I like to learn, do, or change?
  2. My starting point: What experience, skills, or education do I already have?
  3. My barriers: What could make this difficult, such as cost, transportation, health, disability, time, or technology?
  4. My requirements: What documents, qualifications, or application steps are needed?
  5. My first action: What is one manageable thing I can do next?
  6. My backup plan: If this option does not work, which alternative can I investigate?
Privacy first: This page does not need your Social Security number, medical records, passwords, or financial account details. Share sensitive information only when it is required by a legitimate provider through an appropriate, secure process.

Trust, verification, and limitations

Links on this page are intended as starting points, with priority given to official government or established educational resources. A link does not guarantee that a program is accepting applications, has funding, is available in your area, or will determine you eligible.

Before applying, verify current eligibility rules, deadlines, costs, availability, accessibility, and contact details directly with the provider. Avoid paying a third party just to access information that is offered free by an official source.

Career information is general guidance, not a promise of employment, earnings, certification, or admission to a program.
